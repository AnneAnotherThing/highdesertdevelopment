What the work covers

A commercial job is mostly what happens before the building

The structure is rarely the part that slips. The pad is, and the utilities under it, the fire protection, the parking and the approach. Those are the pieces that hold a schedule up, and on most jobs they belong to three or four different companies with no shared reason to care about each other's dates.

Site and underground. Wet and dry utilities. Sewer connections, tie-ins and lift stations. Underground electrical, conduit, pull boxes and backfill. Fire mains in 6, 8, 10 and 12 inch for commercial and industrial projects. Fire hydrants and assemblies. Backflow prevention devices, installed and tested. Trenching, excavation, grading and compaction.

Concrete and the base under the paving. New foundations. Satellite pads. V gutters. Base for parking lots and roads. Concrete parking lots, sidewalks, driveways, curbs and ramps.

Structure and finish. Wood framing, roofing, electrical, plumbing, drywall and paint.

Why one company matters more here than on a house

On a residential build, a gap between two trades costs a week. On a commercial build it costs a tenant or a lease date. The usual reason a job slips is not that anybody did bad work. It is that the trench crew, the concrete crew and the framers each finished on their own schedule and nobody owned the seam between them.

Here those are the same crew. The people who set the fire main are the people who pour the slab over it and the people who frame on top of that. Nobody is working around a stranger's mistake, and nobody is waiting on a subcontractor to call back. The owner is on the site rather than managing it from a phone.

Ground up, tenant improvement, or the site on its own

Not every commercial job is a building. A good share of this work is site packages: the underground and the concrete beneath somebody else's structure, or the parking, approach and drainage on a property that already has its building.

If the building exists and the inside has to change, that is the same trades in a different order. If it is raw ground and a plan, it starts underground. Either way the first conversation is about the site, not the finishes.
