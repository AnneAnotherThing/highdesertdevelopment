Hey Jerry,

You said Altadena is fine and the Palisades area is off limits. Noted, and I will keep it that way. Here is how I would go after the Altadena side of it.

Not by chasing it. There are already plenty of out-of-town operators working those neighbourhoods, and people who have lost a house can spot that a mile off. What actually works is being the one who turns up when somebody sits down and starts searching, which is a different job and one your site is now built for.
Two things I can do

Put Altadena on the map. It is not on your site anywhere at the moment. Adding it to your service area, the town list and the details Google reads is the difference between appearing for somebody searching there and not existing.

A page that answers rebuild questions. Not an advert. The questions people actually type: how it works when the lot already had a house on it, what the insurance side expects from a contractor, permits and debris clearance, and how to check any contractor's licence before signing anything. The same way the vacant lot page works, and that one is the strongest page on your site.
Why you are well placed for it

Licensed since 1999 with the number published, local, the owner on the site, and the trades kept in house. What people are frightened of right now is exactly the opposite of that: an unlicensed crew from out of the area that disappears halfway through. You do not have to claim anything. You just have to be findable and checkable.
Worth knowing before you quote

Rebuild contracts after a declared emergency are treated differently in California, and I would rather you heard it from me than found out on a job. Three things from the licence board:

• The homeowner gets seven days to cancel rather than the usual three.
• No time and materials, and no cost plus on disaster home improvement work. It has to be a fixed contract.
• The deposit is capped at $1,000 or 10 percent of the contract, whichever is less.
